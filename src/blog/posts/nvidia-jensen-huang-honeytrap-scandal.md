On Sunday 8 November 2026, Nvidia employees opened an email with a plain subject line. "A message to our company."

The first lines read like a confession, because they were one. "I owe my family and all of you an apology," Jensen Huang wrote. "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry."

He never named the woman. The reporting did. Her name is Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, a former managing director at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based data-centre company and Nvidia customer. According to *The New York Times*, US intelligence agencies concluded China's Ministry of State Security recruited her to gather information on Nvidia's chief executive. The two are not related, despite the shared surname.

The same day, Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation. This is the story of how the founder of the most powerful company in AI got trapped, why he was the target, and what it cost.

## Who is Alice Huang?

Alice Huang was a senior executive at Megaspeed International, a Singapore-based data-centre company. Megaspeed is described in prior reporting as the largest buyer of Nvidia chips in Southeast Asia — and it is also at the centre of a separate, longer-running allegation: that it diverted Nvidia's advanced AI chips into China in violation of US export controls.

The numbers behind that allegation are large. Malaysian trade records show Megaspeed imported at least 17,544 of Nvidia's GB300 processors — at the time the most advanced AI chips on the market — in November 2025, worth some $787 million. A report by research firm C4ADS linked $4.6 billion in hardware purchases to Megaspeed. The company has categorically denied the chip-diversion allegations, and says Singapore's investigation into alleged chip diversion concluded earlier this year with no adverse action against it.

US authorities have been investigating Megaspeed since 2024. In late summer 2025, Singapore police brought Alice Huang in for questioning and restricted her from leaving the country. She has not been charged with anything. As of 6 November 2026, prosecutors had charged neither Jensen Huang, nor Alice Huang, nor Megaspeed.

That is the first thing to get straight. The affair is admitted. The espionage is alleged — concluded by US intelligence, reported by the *Times*, and denied by China's Foreign Ministry, which called the spy allegation "unsubstantiated". Nothing about it has been proven in court.

## How it happened

The timeline is short, and every step made the next one worse.

**May 2026.** Activist short seller Culper Research described an "uncomfortably close relationship" between Huang and Alice Huang. Almost nobody outside finance noticed.

**Late summer 2025.** Nvidia's general counsel, Tim Teter, called Commerce Under Secretary Jeffrey Kessler — the official who oversees US chip export controls — to ask whether the Bureau of Industry and Security was involved in Singapore's detention of Alice Huang. It was not. Nvidia later said Teter was unaware of the affair when he made the call. But the call itself shows how close the two stories already were: Nvidia's top lawyer was asking Washington about a detained executive at a customer under a smuggling investigation.

**Late 2025.** The Justice Department, working with the Bureau of Industry and Security, asked the bureau to pause its Megaspeed efforts. The bureau has made no progress since, despite having enough evidence to move forward, according to people familiar with the matter.

**29 October 2026.** The *New York Times* published its report: US intelligence had concluded Alice Huang was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security and tasked with gathering information from Nvidia's chief executive while also pushing for broader Chinese access to the company's advanced AI chips. Nvidia called Huang's meetings with her routine contact with a customer. Huang told *Bloomberg* the meetings were "completely normal and not unusual."

**8 November 2026.** Ten days later, Huang admitted the affair in his email to staff. The board opened its investigation the same day, saying it had "no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised" and that Huang had agreed to co-operate fully and would remain in his role.

**9 November 2026.** Nvidia shares fell roughly 7 percent at the open. By mid-morning they traded down 4.25 percent at $178.70 — a drop worth roughly $190 billion in market value. People online started calling it the most expensive affair in history.

**10 November 2026.** Bloomberg reported the Teter intervention, and House Republicans asked Huang for a briefing.

## Why it happened

A honeytrap only works on a target worth trapping. Jensen Huang was worth trapping for four reasons.

**He controls the bottleneck.** Nvidia's chips are the single most important input in AI. Whoever controls access to them controls who can build frontier AI. China is barred from buying the advanced chips without a licence, and US officials believe Chinese firms have bought billions of dollars of banned processors through smuggling networks running through Southeast Asia. Megaspeed sits in the middle of that geography, and Alice Huang sat at the intersection of the customer relationship and the chip-diversion allegation.

**He is the company.** Huang founded Nvidia in 1993, at 30, with two friends in a Silicon Valley diner. He has run it ever since — 33 years, one CEO, no named successor. Nvidia has no publicly disclosed succession plan. When a founder becomes the face of a trillion-dollar empire, a personal scandal is not private drama. It is a product failure. Any shake-up at the top ripples through every AI startup that depends on Nvidia chips, and through the US stock market itself.

**He has the access.** Huang has become a close informal adviser on AI to Donald Trump, travelling with the US President on Air Force One on a trip to Beijing earlier in 2026. That is exactly the kind of access an intelligence service wants to be near — and exactly the kind of access that makes US officials nervous when the person next to him is alleged to be reporting to Beijing.

**He had something to hide.** Huang's marriage sits at the centre of his founder story. He met Lori Mills at Oregon State University — he was 17, she was 19 — and has said he promised her he would be a CEO by 30 so she would marry him. Both of their children work at Nvidia. The image was the devoted family man in the leather jacket. An affair is not just a private failing in that story. It is leverage: something the other side knows, that you need kept quiet, that keeps you coming back to the table.

That is how a trap works. It does not start with secrets. It starts with a relationship, a favour, a dinner, a meeting that is "completely normal and not unusual" — until it is not.

## What it cost

The bill is not only the stock drop. The stock is the smallest part.

**The market.** Roughly 7 percent at the open on 9 November, about $190 billion of value gone in a morning, before a partial recovery. The market was not pricing an affair. It was pricing the possibility that the most important CEO in AI might have to step aside, with no successor named.

**The board.** An independent investigation run by independent directors with outside legal counsel. CNBC sources indicated Huang could take a leave of absence if the investigation interferes with his ability to lead. He remains in his role for now.

**The politics.** House Republicans have asked Huang for a briefing. The company is now answering questions in Washington about its CEO's personal life, its general counsel's phone calls, and its biggest customer in Southeast Asia — all at once.

**The business.** Nvidia has now made chip tracking a condition of its coveted partner status in Southeast Asia, requiring cloud providers to enrol in its Fleet Intelligence system to win or keep Nvidia Cloud Partner status. The enforcement is still unclear — enrolment alone does not force operators to share location data with Nvidia or US authorities — but the direction is plain: the chip-diversion problem is now a board-level problem, not a compliance-department problem.

**The image.** Copyleaks, a company that analyses online content for signs of AI manipulation, said it had seen "an explosion in AI-manipulated photos and videos of Huang and Alice Huang, ranging from memes to more subtle edits." The founder who built his brand on keynote stages and leather jackets is now a meme. That is not a footnote. For a founder whose personal brand is the company's brand, it is a cost.

## What founders should take from this

I am not writing this to gossip about a marriage. I am writing it because every founder who builds a company around their own name should read this story as a warning, not as entertainment.

**You are the single point of failure.** If your company cannot survive a week without you, it cannot survive a scandal about you. Build a successor, a second-in-command, a leadership team that can run the company while you are in the room — and while you are not. Nvidia, the most valuable company in the world, has no named successor after 33 years. That is the real scandal.

**Access is leverage.** Who gets your calendar is a security decision, not a scheduling one. A customer, an investor, a partner who always wants to meet in person, who always has a reason to be close — ask yourself what they get from the proximity. The meetings were "routine contact with a customer" until they were the subject of a board investigation.

**Never lie about a relationship when asked.** Huang told Bloomberg the meetings were completely normal. Ten days later he admitted the affair. The lie did not protect him. It converted a private failing into a credibility problem with the press, the board, and Washington. If you are asked a question you cannot answer honestly, say you cannot answer it. Do not say something untrue.

**Separate the personal from the company — before you have to.** When your marriage, your family, and your children are part of the founder story, a personal failure becomes a company event. That is a choice you make every time you put your private life into the brand. Keep something that belongs only to you.

**Compliance reaches your personal life.** Export controls, sanctions, and foreign-agent rules do not stop at the company door. If your business touches restricted technology, restricted markets, or restricted customers, your personal relationships with people in that world are a compliance matter — not just a social one.

The lesson is not that founders should be paranoid. It is that the bigger you get, the more your personal life is a business risk — and the more you need the structures that survive you.

## Frequently asked questions

### Was Jensen Huang charged with anything?

No. As of 6 November 2026, prosecutors had charged neither Huang, nor Alice Huang, nor Megaspeed. The board's investigation is an internal corporate inquiry, not a criminal case.

### Did Nvidia secrets leak to China?

The board said it has "no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised." US officials did not believe Huang knew Alice Huang might be gathering information for Beijing. That is the board's and the officials' position — the investigation is still running.

### What is a honeytrap?

It is an intelligence term for using a personal or romantic relationship to gain access to a target, gather information, or create leverage over them. The allegation here is that Alice Huang was recruited to get close to Huang. The allegation has not been proven in court.

### Who is Alice Huang, and is she related to Jensen Huang?

She is a former managing director at Megaspeed International, a Singapore-based data-centre company and Nvidia customer. Despite the shared surname, the two are not related.

### What happens next?

The board's independent investigation continues, with outside legal counsel. Huang remains CEO and has agreed to co-operate fully. CNBC sources indicated he could take a leave of absence if the investigation interferes with his ability to lead. House Republicans have asked him for a briefing.
