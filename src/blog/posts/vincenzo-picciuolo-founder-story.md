*A guest founder story by Vincenzo Picciuolo, founder of HORNO Network and OYYO ONE AI.*

There were times when I knew how to solve a difficult technical problem, but I did not know how I was going to pay the next electricity bill.

I could design systems, write code and fix machines. But none of that made the school fees disappear. It did not put food on the table. I had a family to take care of, and some days the responsibility felt heavier than anything I was trying to build.

This is the part of being a founder that people rarely see.

## Where did it start?

My story began long before artificial intelligence. It began when getting online had a sound. I still remember the noise of a modem connecting at 9600 BPS, the waiting, and the excitement of discovering that a small computer could open a door to a much bigger world.

I was curious about everything. How did it work? Why did it work that way? Could I change it? Could I make it better? I made mistakes, broke things, learned to repair them and started again. There was no shortcut. I had to figure things out for myself.

As the years passed, the technology changed. I moved through computers, networks, servers, infrastructure, the internet and new ways of connecting people. During the day I connected machines. During the night I connected people through music. The same curiosity was behind both.

## What is HORNO Network?

Then I started building HORNO Network. I wanted to challenge the idea that digital infrastructure had to depend on a few big companies. What if people could contribute resources to a network and help build something together? What if storage and computing could become more distributed, while privacy and control stayed important?

It was a big idea. Building it was much harder than explaining it.

There were years of work that nobody saw. Problems in the architecture. Code that needed to be rewritten. Decisions that looked right on Monday and were wrong by Friday. I worked, learned, changed things and kept going. Sometimes there was progress. Sometimes I went to bed feeling I had moved nowhere.

And outside the computer, life did not stop. Bills kept arriving. My family still needed me. I knew what it felt like to face an empty wallet and still have a head full of things I wanted to create.

Some people supported me. Others doubted me, dismissed my work or made me feel that I was wasting my time. I listened, because criticism can teach you something. But I learned not to let someone else's opinion become the final decision about my future.

## How do you keep going when quitting would be easier?

There were days when quitting would have been easier. I was tired. I was worried. I did not always know what the next month would look like. What helped me was not some magic confidence. It was finding one thing I could do that day. Fix one problem. Make one call. Improve one part of the system. Take one more step.

That is the whole method, if there is one. Not a grand plan. One step, repeated for years.

If you are building an agency and the work feels invisible, that is normal. We teach the same discipline in [How to Start an AI Automation Agency](/blog/how-to-start-an-ai-automation-agency): pick one niche, build one working demo, sell a result. The tools change every week. The daily step does not.

## Why build OYYO ONE AI?

Later, when AI began to change everything, I saw a different problem. People were surrounded by powerful tools, but using them was becoming complicated. Companies had to move between apps, repeat the same information, learn how to write the right prompts and trust services with data they could not always control.

That is why I began building OYYO ONE AI. I want people to explain what they need in normal words, and let the intelligence understand the goal, organize the work and choose the right models and tools. I want businesses to keep their own knowledge, protect sensitive information and decide what runs locally and what may use the cloud. Privacy, ownership and sovereignty should not be things people have to give up just to use AI.

Today, the OYYO runtime is working and the workspace is running in private beta. We are building a full platform, including the intelligence, models and systems behind it. There is still a lot to do. I would rather be honest about that than pretend the journey is complete.

Sometimes I think about how strange it is. The person who once waited for a 9600 BPS modem to connect is now building complex AI systems. The machines are very different. The reason I sit in front of them is not.

I still want to know what is possible.

## What if you are a founder in a hard season?

If you are a founder reading this while worrying about rent, food, your children or the next bill, I want you to know that a difficult season does not erase your work. A rejection does not erase what you have learned. And the person who laughs at your idea does not get to decide what you become.

Never giving up does not mean ignoring your responsibilities. Take care of your family. Ask for help when you need it. Change your plan if you must. Move more slowly if that is what life allows. Just do not confuse a hard chapter with the end of your story.

I have spent decades learning and building. I have made mistakes. I have faced fear. I have started again more times than I can count. I do not know exactly where the next years will take me.

But I know I am not finished building.
